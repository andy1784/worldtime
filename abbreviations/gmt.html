GMT was set by the astronomical observations at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich. Since 1972 UTC has replaced it as the scientific standard, but GMT survives in everyday British usage and aviation.

GMT quick facts

  • Full name: Greenwich Mean Time
  • Standard offset: UTC+0
  • Daylight saving: BST (UTC+1), late March–late October
  • Where it applies: UK and Ireland in winter; Ghana, Senegal, Iceland year-round; nautical and aviation reference

Frequently asked questions about GMT

What does GMT stand for?

GMT stands for Greenwich Mean Time — mean solar time at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London. It equals UTC+0.

Is GMT the same as UTC?

For practical purposes yes, both are UTC+0. Technically GMT is an astronomical time standard while UTC is kept by atomic clocks with leap-second corrections.

Is London always on GMT?

No. From late March to late October London uses BST (UTC+1).
