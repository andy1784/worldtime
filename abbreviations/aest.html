Australia runs its daylight saving backwards relative to the northern hemisphere: clocks go forward in October and back in April. Queensland skips it entirely, so Sydney–Brisbane gain a phantom hour half the year.

AEST quick facts

  • Full name: Australian Eastern Standard Time
  • Standard offset: UTC+10
  • Daylight saving: AEDT (UTC+11), October–April (NSW, VIC, SA, TAS only)
  • Where it applies: Queensland year-round; NSW, Victoria, Tasmania, ACT in winter months

Frequently asked questions about AEST

What does AEST stand for?

AEST stands for Australian Eastern Standard Time, UTC+10, covering Queensland year-round and the southeastern states outside daylight-saving months.

What is AEDT?

Australian Eastern Daylight Time, UTC+11 — AEST shifted forward from October to April.

Do Sydney and Brisbane always share a clock?

No. During Australian summer Sydney is on AEDT while Brisbane stays on AEST, creating a one-hour gap.
