Alaska moved to UTC−9 in 1983, merging four old zones into one. Because of extreme northern latitude, Anchorage gets over 19 hours of daylight in June — DST barely registers against that swing.

AKST quick facts

  • Full name: Alaska Standard Time
  • Standard offset: UTC−9
  • Daylight saving: AKDT (UTC−8), March–November
  • Where it applies: State of Alaska (most of it)

Frequently asked questions about AKST

What does AKST stand for?

AKST stands for Alaska Standard Time, UTC−9, used across most of Alaska.

What is AKDT?

Alaska Daylight Time, UTC−8 — the summer variant.

Why is Alaska only one hour behind Pacific Time?

Congress consolidated Alaska into a single zone in 1983; before that the state stretched across four different offsets.
