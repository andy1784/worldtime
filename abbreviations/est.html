EST is the most-searched time-zone abbreviation in the world. It covers roughly 47% of the US population. In everyday speech people say "EST" year-round, but from March to November the zone technically switches to EDT.

EST quick facts

  • Full name: Eastern Standard Time
  • Standard offset: UTC−5
  • Daylight saving: EDT (UTC−4), March–November
  • Where it applies: US East Coast (New York, Washington DC, Miami), Ontario, Quebec, Colombia, Peru, Ecuador, Jamaica, Panama

Frequently asked questions about EST

What does EST stand for?

EST stands for Eastern Standard Time, UTC−5. It is used on the US East Coast and in parts of Canada, South America and the Caribbean.

Is New York currently EST or EDT?

From early November to mid-March New York is on EST (UTC−5). From March to November it observes daylight saving as EDT (UTC−4).

What is the difference between EST and EDT?

EDT is Eastern Daylight Time — the same zone shifted one hour forward for daylight saving. EST is UTC−5; EDT is UTC−4.
