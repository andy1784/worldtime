🇦🇺 Time Zones in Australia

Australia has three main time zones: Eastern (AEST, UTC+10), Central (ACST, UTC+9:30), and Western (AWST, UTC+8). During DST, the east coast moves to AEDT (UTC+11), creating a 3.5-hour gap between Sydney and Perth. Australia's DST schedule is opposite to the Northern Hemisphere — summer DST runs from October to April.

Time in Daily Life

Australia's three time zones mean a video call between Perth (AWST, UTC+8) and Sydney (AEST, UTC+10) easily spans two or three hours, and during summer the gap widens further when the east adopts daylight saving while Western Australia stays put.

In Sydney and Melbourne, the working day usually runs 09:00 to 17:00, but the country's size means Perth is already winding down as the east coast is still at lunch. States like Queensland and Western Australia have repeatedly rejected daylight saving in public votes, preferring consistent clocks.

Australian broadcast and rail schedules are built around the state boundaries, so travellers crossing from South Australia into New South Wales must reset their watches by 30 minutes even though the drive is short.
