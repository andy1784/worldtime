🇰🇵 Time in North Korea

North Korea currently uses Korean Standard Time (KST), which is UTC+9:00 — the same as South Korea. But between 2015 and 2018, the country used its own "Pyongyang Time" at UTC+8:30. The change was a political statement: North Korea created Pyongyang Time to break away from what it called a legacy of Japanese colonial rule (Japan occupied Korea from 1910 to 1945 and set the time zone). In 2018, Kim Jong-un abolished Pyongyang Time and reverted to UTC+9:00 as a gesture of reconciliation with South Korea. Pyongyang, the capital, is the largest city in the country.

Time in Daily Life

North Korea uses UTC+9, the same as South Korea and Japan, but since 2015 it has kept a fixed 'Pyongyang Time' with no daylight saving.

The country set its clock back by 30 minutes in 2015 to mark a break from the Japanese colonial era, then realigned with the south in 2018 as a gesture of reconciliation.

Daily life follows strict state scheduling, with mass events, broadcasts, and work brigades all keyed to the single national clock.

The mass gymnastics and parades keyed to national holidays like the Party Foundation Day draw choreographed crowds, and the daily schedule bends around the state broadcast calendar.
