Time in Daily Life

Taiwan uses UTC+8 year-round with no daylight saving, so Taipei, Kaohsiung, and Taichung all share one national clock.

The country has kept a fixed offset for decades, which simplifies business and broadcast schedules across the island.

The subtropical climate keeps day length fairly steady, and night markets — open well past 21:00 — are a centrepiece of evening social life.

The Lunar New Year is the main family migration of the year, and the summer's lantern festival and the year-round night-market culture keep the streets busy past 22:00.

The high-speed rail from Taipei to Kaohsiung covers the island in under two hours, all on UTC+8, so a business meeting can span north and south without a clock change.
