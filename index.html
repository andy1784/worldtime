Understanding Time Zones

Time zones are regions of Earth that observe uniform standard time for legal, commercial, and social purposes. The world is divided into 24 time zones based on UTC (Coordinated Universal Time), also known as GMT (Greenwich Mean Time). Daylight Saving Time (DST) is observed in many countries to make better use of daylight hours during warmer months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time is it in New York right now?

New York is in the Eastern Time Zone (ET). During standard time, New York uses EST (UTC-5). During daylight saving time (March-November), New York uses EDT (UTC-4). Use our live world clock to see the exact current time in New York.

What time is it in London right now?

London is in the GMT/BST time zone. During winter, London uses Greenwich Mean Time (GMT/UTC+0). During summer (late March to late October), London uses British Summer Time (BST/UTC+1).

What is the time difference between New York and London?

The time difference between New York and London is typically 5 hours, with London being ahead. However, this can vary by 1 hour during DST transitions since the US and UK change clocks on different dates.

What time is it in Tokyo, Japan?

Tokyo uses Japan Standard Time (JST), which is UTC+9. Japan does not observe daylight saving time, so Tokyo maintains the same UTC offset year-round.

How do time zones work?

Time zones are regions that observe the same standard time. The world is divided into 24 time zones, each roughly 15 degrees of longitude wide. UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the primary time standard.

What is Daylight Saving Time (DST)?

Daylight Saving Time (DST) is the practice of advancing clocks by one hour during warmer months so that darkness falls at a later clock time. Not all countries observe DST.

Which countries do not observe daylight saving time?

Most countries in Africa, Asia, and South America do not observe DST. Notable examples include Japan, China, India, Brazil, Argentina, and all of Africa except Morocco and Namibia. The European Union has also voted to abolish seasonal clock changes, though implementation has been delayed.

How do I schedule a meeting across multiple time zones?

Use our meeting planner to find overlapping business hours across cities. A good rule of thumb: aim for 9 AM–11 AM in the earliest time zone, which typically falls within business hours for most participants. Avoid scheduling meetings outside 8 AM–6 PM local time for any participant.

What is UTC and how is it different from GMT?

UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the primary time standard by which the world regulates clocks. GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) is a time zone used in the UK and West Africa. For most practical purposes, UTC and GMT show the same time (UTC+0), but UTC is based on atomic clocks while GMT is based on solar time at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich.

Why do some time zones have 30-minute offsets?

Some regions use half-hour offsets for geographical or political reasons. India uses UTC+5:30, Iran uses UTC+3:30, Afghanistan uses UTC+4:30, and Nepal uses UTC+5:45. These offsets often reflect a compromise between neighboring time zones or alignment with the region's solar noon.
