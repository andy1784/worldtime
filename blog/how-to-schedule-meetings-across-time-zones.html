How to Schedule Meetings Across Time Zones Without Burning Out Your Team

📅 August 19, 2026  ·  ⏱ 8 min read  ·  🏷 Remote Work, Time Zones

Your design lead is in São Paulo. Your backend engineer is in Bangalore. Your product manager is in London. And you're in New York trying to find a time for a 30-minute sync that doesn't make someone wake up at 6 AM or stay up past midnight.

Sound familiar? If you manage a distributed team, this is your daily reality. The good news: it doesn't have to be this painful.

The Golden Rule: 9-11 AM in the Earliest Time Zone

Here's the simplest heuristic that works 80% of the time: schedule your meeting for 9-11 AM in the earliest participant's time zone.

Why? Because 9 AM in Tokyo (UTC+9) is midnight in New York (UTC-5) — impossible. But 9 AM in New York is 10 PM in Tokyo — also impossible. The sweet spot only exists when the spread isn't extreme.

Let's break down what "9-11 AM earliest" actually looks like for common team distributions:

US + EuropeNew York (ET)9:00 AM ET2:00 PM London ✓

US West + AsiaLos Angeles (PT)9:00 AM PT1:00 AM+1 Tokyo ✗

Green checkmarks = workable. Red X = someone's sleeping. If your spread hits a red X, you have three options — and "make them deal with it" isn't one of them.

Option 1: Rotate the Pain (Fairness Over Convenience)

If your team spans 12+ hours (like US West Coast + Singapore), there is no "good" time. Someone will be outside 8 AM-6 PM. The only fair approach: rotate.

  • Month 1: 7 AM PT / 10 PM SGT — Asia takes the hit
  • Month 2: 7 PM PT / 10 AM SGT — US takes the hit
  • Month 3: 11 PM PT / 2 PM SGT — Split the difference

Put it on the calendar as a recurring event with "Rotating — see description" so everyone knows the deal. No surprises, no resentment.

Option 2: Async-First, Sync-Last

Ask yourself: Does this actually need to be a meeting?

Most "sync" meetings fall into three categories that work better async:

  • Status updates → Write a weekly thread in Slack/Notion/Linear. Everyone reads on their own time.
  • Design reviews → Record a Loom video walking through the Figma file. Collect feedback in comments.
  • Decision-making → Write a one-pager with context, options, and your recommendation. Ask for comments by EOD.

Reserve synchronous time only for: relationship building (1:1s, team bonding), complex problem-solving with lots of back-and-forth, and genuine emergencies.

Option 3: Split the Meeting

If you have a 60-minute agenda and a 14-hour spread, consider two 30-minute sessions:

  • Session A: 8 AM PT / 11 PM SGT (US-friendly)
  • Session B: 7 PM PT / 10 AM SGT (Asia-friendly)
  • Share notes/recordings between sessions

Yes, it means you (the organizer) attend both. That's the cost of fairness.

Practical Etiquette Rules

  1. Always include time zones in invites. "2 PM ET / 7 PM BST / 11:30 PM IST" — no ambiguity.
  2. Set working hours in your calendar. Google Calendar, Outlook, and Calendly all support this. Let the tool enforce boundaries.
  3. Record every meeting. Someone will miss it. Make the recording + transcript easy to find.
  4. Send an agenda 24h ahead. If there's no agenda, there's no meeting.
  5. End on time. Running over is disrespectful when someone's already outside their normal hours.

The "Follow the Sun" Model (For 24/7 Coverage)

If you're building a support or ops team that needs 24/7 coverage, don't make individuals cover odd hours. Hire in three regions:

  • Americas (UTC-8 to UTC-4)
  • EMEA (UTC+0 to UTC+3)
  • APAC (UTC+8 to UTC+11)

Each region works their local 9-5. Handovers happen at overlap windows. No one works nights. This is how GitLab, Zapier, and other fully remote companies operate at scale.

Quick Checklist Before You Send That Invite

  • ☐ Used the Meeting Planner to verify overlap?
  • ☐ Time zone written out in the invite (not just "2 PM")?
  • ☐ No one outside 8 AM-6 PM their local time? (If yes → rotate or split)
  • ☐ Agenda attached?
  • ☐ Recording will be available?
  • ☐ Async alternative considered first?

Final Thought

Time zones aren't a technical problem — they're a human one. The tools exist. The math is solved. What's missing is the discipline to say "this meeting isn't worth making someone wake up at 5 AM" and the empathy to rotate when there's no perfect answer.

Your team will remember the meetings that respected their sleep. They'll also remember the ones that didn't.
