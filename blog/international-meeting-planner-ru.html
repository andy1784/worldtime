Coordinating meetings across borders shouldn't require a spreadsheet. The right tools and habits turn time zone chaos into a smooth workflow.

What to Look for in a Meeting Planner

A good international meeting planner does three things:

  1. Visualizes overlap: Shows working hours for each participant side by side
  2. Handles DST: Automatically adjusts for daylight saving transitions
  3. Exports clean invites: Generates calendar invites with UTC + 3 local times

Our Built-In Tools

  • Meeting Planner — drag cities, see overlap instantly, get a calendar-ready invite line
  • Time Difference Calculator — quick two-city conversion with DST awareness
  • World Time Map — visual day/night map showing business hours globally

Workflow: From "When?" to "Booked" in 60 Seconds

  1. Open the Meeting Planner
  2. Add each attendee's city
  3. Drag the time slider to find green (everyone in 08:00–18:00)
  4. Click "Copy Invite" — gets you "15:00 UTC (08:00 SF / 16:00 London / 23:00 Tokyo)"
  5. Paste into calendar invite

Advanced: Recurring Meeting Templates

For standing meetings, create a template with the rotation rule embedded:

"Weekly Sync - Week A: 14:00 UTC (06:00 LA / 14:00 London / 22:00 Singapore) | Week B: 22:00 UTC (14:00 LA / 22:00 London / 06:00 Singapore). Rotation flips every sprint."

Paste this into the recurring event description. Everyone knows the pattern.

When Tools Aren't Enough

If your team spans >12 hours (e.g., Americas + Asia), no tool finds a good window. The solution isn't better software — it's async-first culture. Replace the recurring call with a weekly written update + 15-min optional live chat. The teams that do this well ship faster because they respect focus time.
