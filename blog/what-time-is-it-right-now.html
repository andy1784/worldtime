Understanding Time Zones

The world is divided into 24 time zones, each roughly 15 degrees of longitude wide. The reference point is UTC (Coordinated Universal Time), which replaced GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) as the global standard.

Here are the major time zones and their UTC offsets:

  • EST (Eastern Standard Time) — UTC-5 — New York, Toronto, Miami
  • CST (Central Standard Time) — UTC-6 — Chicago, Dallas, Mexico City
  • MST (Mountain Standard Time) — UTC-7 — Denver, Phoenix, Salt Lake City
  • PST (Pacific Standard Time) — UTC-8 — Los Angeles, San Francisco, Seattle
  • GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) — UTC+0 — London, Dublin, Lisbon
  • CET (Central European Time) — UTC+1 — Paris, Berlin, Rome
  • EET (Eastern European Time) — UTC+2 — Athens, Helsinki, Cairo
  • IST (India Standard Time) — UTC+5:30 — Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore
  • CST (China Standard Time) — UTC+8 — Beijing, Shanghai, Hong Kong
  • JST (Japan Standard Time) — UTC+9 — Tokyo, Osaka
  • AEDT (Australian Eastern) — UTC+11 — Sydney, Melbourne

Daylight Saving Time: Why Times Change

Many countries observe Daylight Saving Time (DST), where clocks move forward by one hour during summer months. This means the UTC offset of a city can change depending on the time of year.

For example, New York is UTC-5 (EST) in winter but UTC-4 (EDT) in summer. London is UTC+0 (GMT) in winter but UTC+1 (BST) in summer. Our world clock automatically adjusts for DST — you always get the correct current time.
