跨时区会议指南：在不耗尽团队精力的情况下安排会议

📅 July 16, 2026  ·  ⏱ 6 min read  ·  🏷 time zones

📅 July 10, 2026 ·  ⏱ 6 min read ·  🏷 Time Zones, Guides, Productivity

Scheduling across time zones is one of the hardest parts of remote work. Done poorly, it burns out team members in one region. Done well, it becomes a competitive advantage — your team runs 24/7 without anyone working nights.

Start with Async, Not a Meeting

Before you reach for the calendar, ask: does this need to be live? Most status updates, code reviews, and design feedback work better asynchronously. Written communication creates a searchable record, respects focus time, and works across any time zone gap.

Reserve live calls for: relationship building, complex decisions with many stakeholders, and genuine brainstorming where real-time interaction adds value.

Map Your Team's Overlap Windows

List every team member's city and working hours (e.g., 09:00–17:00 local). Convert all to UTC. The intersection is your overlap window. For a team in SF (UTC-8), London (UTC+0), and Singapore (UTC+8):

  • SF 09:00 = 17:00 UTC = 01:00 Singapore (next day) — too late
  • SF 16:00 = 00:00 UTC = 08:00 Singapore — edge of day
  • Best window: 15:00–16:00 UTC (07:00–08:00 SF / 15:00–16:00 London / 23:00–00:00 Singapore)

This is tight. If Singapore is optional, 13:00–15:00 UTC works better.

Rotate the Pain Fairly

When no window works for everyone, rotate which region takes the inconvenience:

  • Sprint 1: APAC-friendly (morning APAC, evening Americas)
  • Sprint 2: EMEA-friendly (morning EMEA, evening APAC)
  • Sprint 3: Americas-friendly (morning Americas, evening EMEA)

Publish the rotation schedule at the start of each quarter. Predictability lets people plan their lives.

Use UTC in Every Invite

Never send "9 AM meeting" — always "15:00 UTC (07:00 LA / 15:00 London / 23:00 Singapore)." The UTC anchor is unambiguous; local times are just courtesy conversions.

Handle DST Transitions Proactively

Mark the US/EU transition weeks on the team calendar (March and November). During those 2–3 weeks, overlaps shift by an hour. Decide in advance: fixed UTC (easier during transition) or fixed local time (easier rest of year). Document the rule.
