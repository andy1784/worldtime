UTCオフセット別国リスト（ 2026年）

If you have ever tried to figure out what time it is in some other country, the first thing you check is its UTC offset. This page is a single-reference list of every country in the world, grouped by that offset — from the westernmost inhabited places on UTC-11 all the way out to the Line Islands at UTC+14.

Offsets below are standard time. Countries that observe daylight saving time are marked (DST); their summer offset is shifted one hour forward. Where a country uses a half-hour or quarter-hour offset, we mark it accordingly.

Spoiler: the world is not a clean grid of hourly zones. A handful of countries use 30-minute or even 45-minute offsets, and a handful of large countries span so many degrees of longitude that they officially run more than one clock. See Why Some Countries Use 30-Minute Offsets and Why China Has Only One Time Zone for the backstory on those edge cases.

UTC-12 and UTC-11

Only two inhabited regions sit this far west of Greenwich. After Baker Island and Howland Island (UTC-12, uninhabited), the next populated step west is American Samoa and the US Minor Outlying Islands.

  • UTC-12: Baker Island, Howland Island (United States Minor Outlying Islands — uninhabited) — purely a date-line reference.
  • UTC-11: American Samoa, Midway Atoll, Niue.

UTC-10 and UTC-9

Hawaii, Alaska, French Polynesia and the Cook Islands anchor the second tier of the planet’s western clocks.

  • UTC-10: Cook Islands, French Polynesia (Society Islands: Tahiti, Moorea; Tuamotu; Marquesas NOT — see UTC-9:30), United States (Hawaii, Aleutian Islands west of 169°30′W observe Alaska DST and shift to UTC-9 in summer; Hawaii does not observe DST).
  • UTC-9: French Polynesia (Marquesas Islands — actually UTC-9:30, listed under half-hour below), United States (Alaska — DST: UTC-8 in summer).
  • UTC-9 (winter) / UTC-8 (summer): France (Gambier Islands).

UTC-8 and UTC-7

The Pacific coast of North America keeps UTC-8 year-round in places that have abolished DST, while UTC-7 covers the Mountain belt.

  • UTC-8: Canada (British Columbia: most of province observes DST; Cranbrook and some east Kootenay areas remain MST year-round — UTC-7), Mexico (Baja California — formerly UTC-8, now follows California DST), United States (Pacific — California, Oregon, Washington, Nevada west of 115°W: DST in summer, when it becomes UTC-7), Pitcairn Islands (UK — UTC-8 year-round, no DST).
  • UTC-7: Canada (Alberta, Northwest Territories, Nunavut west of 85°W, Saskatchewan except Lloydminster — Saskatchewan does not observe DST and stays on UTC-6), Mexico (Chihuahua, Sonora: Sonora abandoned DST in 1999 and stays on UTC-7 year-round; Chihuahua follows US Mountain DST, oscillating between UTC-7 and UTC-6), United States (Mountain — Arizona except Navajo Nation observes UTC-7 year-round with no DST; the rest of Mountain observes DST, shifting to UTC-6 in summer).

UTC-6 and UTC-5

Central and Eastern North America, plus a broad slice of Central America and the Caribbean.

  • UTC-6: Belize, Canada (Manitoba, Nunavut central, Ontario west of 90°W, Saskatchewan — DST except Saskatchewan), Costa Rica, El Salvador, Ecuador (mainland — no DST), Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico (most of the country, uses DST), Nicaragua, United States (Central — DST shifting to UTC-5 in summer).
  • UTC-5: Bahamas (DST shifts to UTC-4 in summer), Canada (Ontario east of 90°W, Quebec — DST), Cayman Islands, Colombia (no DST), Cuba (DST — UTC-4 in summer), Ecuador (Galapagos is UTC-6, not UTC-5; mainland UTC-5), Haiti (DST shifting to UTC-4 in summer since 2017), Honduras listed UTC-6 above, Jamaica (no DST), Mexico (Quintana Roo — UTC-5 year-round, no DST), Panama (no DST), Peru (no DST), Turks and Caicos (DST shifting to UTC-4 in summer since 2018), United States (Eastern — DST shifting to UTC-4 in summer).

UTC-4 and UTC-3

Atlantic Canada, the Caribbean, and most of South America live in this band.

  • UTC-4: Angola (no DST), Antigua and Barbuda, Aruba, Barbados, Bermuda (DST, shifts to UTC-3 in summer), Bolivia (no DST), Brazil (Amazon region and several eastern states formerly UTC-4; Amazonas, Roraima, Rondônia are UTC-4 year-round), Canada (Atlantic — New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island — DST shifts to UTC-3 in summer), Chile (DST shifts to UTC-3 in summer), Curaçao, Dominica, Dominican Republic, Falkland Islands (DST: UTC-3 in summer), Grenada, Guyana, Paraguay (DST: UTC-3 in summer), Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Sint Maarten, Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago, Venezuela (no DST — reverted in 2016 from UTC-4:30).
  • UTC-3: Argentina (no DST since 2009), Brazil (Brasília time — most populous zone: UTC-3; no DST nationwide since 2019), France (French Guiana, Saint Pierre and Miquelon — SPM observes DST), Greenland (most of the populated coast — DST shifts to UTC-2 in summer), Saint Barthélemy, Saint Martin (French side).

UTC-2 and UTC-1

Open ocean and a handful of Atlantic islands.

  • UTC-2: Brazil (Fernando de Noronha), Greenland (Ittoqqortoormiit region — DST in summer)
  • UTC-1: Cape Verde, Greenland (Nunap Isuataa, Uummannaq — uses Atlantic DST, shifting to UTC+0 in summer), Iceland (no DST; Iceland wound clocks back in 1968 and has been fixed at UTC+0 since then for practical purposes, though nominally it sits in UTC-1/UTC+0 with European DST), Portugal (Azores — DST, shifts to UTC+0 in summer).

UTC+0 (GMT)

UTC+0 runs through Western Europe, the British Isles and West Africa.

  • UTC+0: Burkina Faso, The Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Iceland (de facto UTC+0; nominally UTC-1/UTC+0 with European DST but did not switch), Ireland (DST: UTC+1 in summer — same as UK), Liberia, Mali, Mauritania, Portugal (mainland — DST: UTC+1 in summer), São Tomé and Príncipe, Senegal, Sierra Leone, Togo, United Kingdom (DST: UTC+1 in summer).

UTC+1 and UTC+2

Central Europe and most of continental Africa sit here.

  • UTC+1: Albania (DST: UTC+2), Algeria, Andorra (DST: UTC+2), Angola was previously UTC+1, now UTC+1 — corrected, listed above as UTC-1 erroneously above; current is moot, see UTC+1 in WAT band), Austria (DST: UTC+2), Belgium (DST: UTC+2), Benin, Bosnia and Herzegovina (DST: UTC+2), Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, Republic of the Congo, Croatia (DST: UTC+2), Czechia (DST: UTC+2), Denmark (DST: UTC+2), Equatorial Guinea, France (DST: UTC+2), Gabon, Germany (DST: UTC+2), Gibraltar (UK, DST: UTC+2), Hungary (DST: UTC+2), Italy (DST: UTC+2), Kosovo (DST: UTC+2), Luxembourg (DST: UTC+2), Malta (DST: UTC+2), Monaco (DST: UTC+2), Montenegro (DST: UTC+2), Morocco (no DST since 2018 since reinstated recently with a permanent shift — UTC+1 year-round), Netherlands (DST: UTC+2), Niger, Nigeria, North Macedonia (DST: UTC+2), Norway (DST: UTC+2), Poland (DST: UTC+2), San Marino (DST: UTC+2), Serbia (DST: UTC+2), Slovakia (DST: UTC+2), Slovenia (DST: UTC+2), Spain (DST: UTC+2; see Why Spain is in the Wrong Time Zone), Sweden (DST: UTC+2), Switzerland (DST: UTC+2), Tunisia, Vatican City (Holy See) (DST: UTC+2), Western Sahara.
  • UTC+2: Botswana, Bulgaria (DST since 2025 restoring EU-aligned clocks), Burundi, Cyprus (DST: UTC+3), Democratic Republic of the Congo (eastern: Lubumbashi, South Kivu, North Kivu, etc.), Egypt (no DST — fixed on UTC+2 since 2014; previously shifted to UTC+3 in summer), Eswatini, Estonia (no DST; Estonia uses EU-aligned DST fixes each March-November: UTC+2/UTC+3), Finland (DST: UTC+3), Greece (DST: UTC+3), Israel (DST: UTC+3), Jordan (DST: UTC+3), Latvia (DST: UTC+3), Lebanon (DST: UTC+3 since 2023 rule change), Lesotho, Libya, Lithuania (DST: UTC+3), Malawi, Moldova (DST: UTC+3 since 2025 alignment), Mozambique, Namibia (DST: UTC+2 in winter, UTC+1 in winter — reversed notation: Namibia uses UTC+2 standard / UTC+1 winter), Romania (DST: UTC+3 since 2025 alignment; previously permanent UTC+2 for several years), Russia (Kaliningrad — no DST), Rwanda, South Africa (no DST), South Sudan, Sudan, Syria (DST: UTC+3), Türkiye (DST nominally; in practice on UTC+3 year-round since 2016), Ukraine (DST: UTC+3 — see Ukraine Time Zone), Zambia, Zimbabwe.

UTC+3 and UTC+4

The Middle East and Eastern Europe form UTC+3; the Gulf and Caucasus anchor UTC+4.

  • UTC+3: Bahrain, Belarus (no DST), Comoros, Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Georgia (no DST), Iraq, Kenya, Kuwait, Madagascar, Mayotte (France), Qatar, Russia (Moscow — no DST), Saudi Arabia, Somalia, Tanzania, Uganda, United Arab Emirates, Yemen.
  • UTC+4: Armenia (no DST), Azerbaijan (no DST), Georgia listed above as UTC+3 — corrected: Georgia is UTC+4 year-round since 2004, no DST), Mauritius (UTC+4 year-round; no DST since 2008), Oman, Russia (Samara: UTC+4 year-round), Réunion (France), Seychelles, United Arab Emirates listed above as UTC+3 — corrected: UAE is UTC+4 year-round).

UTC+5 and UTC+6

Central Asia, the Indian subcontinent, and Bangladesh/Bhutan. India is the largest user of a half-hour offset in this band.

  • UTC+5: Antarctica (some research bases including Vostok), French Southern Territories (Adélie Land / Dumont d’Urville and the Iles Eparses in the Mozambique Channel), Heard and McDonald Islands (Australian territory), Kazakhstan (western parts — Mangystau, Atyrau — UTC+5 year-round; the rest of Kazakhstan is UTC+6), Maldives, Pakistan (UTC+5 year-round since 2010), Russia (Yekaterinburg: UTC+5 year-round), Tajikistan, Turkmenistan (UTC+5 year-round), Uzbekistan (UTC+5 year-round).
  • UTC+5:30: India, Sri Lanka (note the unique 30-minute offset — see Best Time to Call India).
  • UTC+5:45: Nepal (one of only two 45-minute offsets on Earth).
  • UTC+6: Antarctica (Vostok Station shifted), Bangladesh, Bhutan (UTC+6 year-round), British Indian Ocean Territory, Kazakhstan (most of the country), Kyrgyzstan, Russia (Omsk: UTC+6 year-round).

UTC+7 and UTC+8

Indochina, most of Mongolia, western China and the China-time belt of Russia all share this window. China nominally covers the entire UTC+8 band even though its territory spans five hours of solar time.

  • UTC+7: Antarctica (some bases), Cambodia (UTC+7 year-round, no DST), Indonesia (western three hours: Sumatra, Java, West and Central Kalimantan — UTC+7), Laos, Mongolia (western provinces: Hovd, Bayan-Ölgii, Uvs, Zavkhan, Khovd, Govi-Altai — UTC+7), Russia (Krasnoyarsk / Kemerovo / Tomsk, Novosibirsk switched to UTC+7 in 2016, Altai and Altai Krai aligned UTC+7 in 2016), Thailand (UTC+7 year-round, no DST), Vietnam (UTC+7 year-round, no DST).
  • UTC+8: Australia (Western Australia — Perth region, no DST), Brunei (UTC+8 year-round), China (entire country uses UTC+8 — see China’s One Time Zone), Hong Kong (UTC+8, no DST), Indonesia (Central Kalimantan, South Kalimantan, Sulawesi, Lesser Sunda Islands — UTC+8), Macau (UTC+8, no DST), Malaysia (UTC+8, no DST), Mongolia (most of country — UTC+8, no DST), Philippines (UTC+8, no DST), Russia (Irkutsk: UTC+8 year-round), Singapore (UTC+8, no DST), Taiwan (UTC+8, no DST), Western Australia (see above).

UTC+9 and UTC+10

Korea and Japan, eastern Russia, the entire Australian Eastern seaboard, and Papua New Guinea.

  • UTC+9: East Timor (Timor-Leste: UTC+9 year-round), Indonesia (Maluku Islands, Papua and West Papua — UTC+9), Japan (UTC+9 year-round — no DST, see Japan’s One-Timezone Paradox), North Korea (UTC+9 since May 2018, when it "reunified" clocks with South Korea), Palau (UTC+9 year-round), Russia (Yakutsk: UTC+9 year-round), South Korea (UTC+9 year-round, no DST since 1988).
  • UTC+9:30: Australia (South Australia, Northern Territory — DST: UTC+10:30 in summer).
  • UTC+10: Antarctica (Casey Station — DST shifts), Australia (New South Wales except Broken Hill, Victoria, Tasmania, Queensland, Australian Capital Territory — see DST notes: NSW, Vic, Tasmania, ACT observe DST, becoming UTC+11 in summer; Queensland and Broken Hill do not), Guam (US territory — UTC+10, no DST), Northern Mariana Islands (UTC+10, no DST), Papua New Guinea (UTC+10 year-round), Russia (Vladivostok: UTC+10 year-round).
  • UTC+10:30: Australia (Lord Howe Island — DST: UTC+11 in summer).

UTC+11 and UTC+12

The western Pacific plus the International Date Line.

  • UTC+11: New Caledonia (France — UTC+11 year-round), Russia (Magadan, Sakhalin Oblast, Kamchatka, Chukotka — UTC+11 year-round), Solomon Islands (UTC+11 year-round), Vanuatu (UTC+11 year-round).
  • UTC+12: Fiji (DST: UTC+13 in southern summer), France (Wallis and Futuna — UTC+12 year-round), Kiribati (Gilbert Islands — actually UTC+12; Banaba is UTC+11), Marshall Islands (UTC+12 year-round), Nauru (UTC+12 year-round), New Zealand (DST: UTC+13 in southern summer), Russia (Kamchatka runs UTC+12 year-round — some sources list UTC+11 in connection with Chukotka), Tuvalu (UTC+12 year-round), United States Minor Outlying Islands (Wake Island — UTC+12).
  • UTC+12:45: New Zealand (Chatham Islands — DST: UTC+13:45 in summer).

UTC+13 and UTC+14

A small number of Pacific countries intentionally sit a day ahead of their neighbours to keep the calendar aligned with trading partners.

  • UTC+13: Kiribati (Phoenix Islands — UTC+13 year-round, intentionally separated from the Gilbert Islands in 1995), Samoa (UTC+13 year-round since 2011 — see What is the International Date Line?), Tokelau (UTC+13 year-round, switched with Samoa in 2011), Tonga (UTC+13 year-round).
  • UTC+14: Kiribati (Line Islands, including Kiritimati — UTC+14 year-round; the first country on Earth to see the new day).

Half-hour and 45-minute offsets

Most of the world aligns to whole hours, but a few countries and territories buck the pattern. Half-hour offsets keep solar time close across wider portions of national territory; the 45-minute offsets are deliberate political statements.

  • UTC+5:30 — India, Sri Lanka.
  • UTC+4:30 — Afghanistan, Iran (summer DST).
  • UTC+9:30 — Australia (South Australia, Northern Territory).
  • UTC+10:30 — Lord Howe Island (Australia).
  • UTC+5:45 — Nepal.
  • UTC+12:45 — Chatham Islands (New Zealand).
  • UTC-3:30 — Newfoundland and Labrador (Canada) — DST shifts to UTC-2:30 in summer.
  • UTC-9:30 — Marquesas Islands (French Polynesia).

See the dedicated deep-dive: Why Some Countries Use 30-Minute Time Zone Offsets.

How countries choose their UTC offset

There is no global authority that assigns UTC offsets — each sovereign country decides for itself. In practice three forces dominate:

  1. Longitude. One hour of solar time equals 15° of longitude, so an offset roughly tracks the geographic centre of a country’s land mass.
  2. Politics. Countries sometimes pick an offset to stay aligned with neighbours, ex-colonial powers, or major trading partners. China put its whole country on UTC+8 in 1949; India stays on UTC+5:30 partly to differentiate itself from both Pakistan (UTC+5) and Bangladesh (UTC+6); the entire Pacific Island line of UTC+12/UTC+13/UTC+14 was created when date-line countries shifted east.
  3. Daylight saving. Roughly 70 countries shift one hour forward during part of the year. The European Union abolished the seasonal switch in 2026 — see Daylight Saving 2026 Prep — but most non-EU countries and US states will continue for now.

Therefore the table above shows standard time; during DST the offset of any marked country shifts one hour forward in summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many countries are in the world in 2026?

There are 195 sovereign states: 193 members of the United Nations plus the Holy See (Vatican City) and the State of Palestine. Some lists count 197 if you include Taiwan and the Cook Islands separately.

Which country has the most UTC offsets inside it?

France technically spans twelve UTC offsets because of its overseas territories (mainland Europe, Guadeloupe, Guyane, Saint-Pierre-and-Miquelon, Mayotte, Réunion, New Caledonia, Wallis and Futuna, French Polynesia, Crozet/Adélie and the Éparses).

Which country uses UTC+14?

Kiribati’s Line Islands — including Kiritimati (Christmas Island). It is the first inhabited place on Earth to see each new day.

Why is the world divided into 24 hours and not more?

The 24-hour system was settled by the International Meridian Conference in 1884. The choice was practical: it divides Earth’s 360° rotation into 15° steps, which is convenient for navigation.

Which countries do not observe daylight saving time?

Most of Africa, all of equatorial Asia, and a handful of countries with extreme latitudes (Japan, China, Singapore, Korea, Iceland, Russia since 2014, Türkiye since 2016). The EU stopped the seasonal change in 2026.

What is the difference between UTC, GMT and Zulu?

UTC is the modern atomic-based reference that replaced GMT in 1972. GMT is now the civil time zone for UK and several West African countries. "Zulu" (Z) is the NATO phonetic way to say UTC+0.

Will the EU keep UTC+1 / UTC+2 in 2026?

The EU clock directive abolishes the spring and autumn clock changes starting 2026. Each country will pick either permanent UTC+1 or UTC+2 once they formally opt in.

2026年、世界にはいくつの国がありますか？

195の主権国家があります。193の国連加盟国に加えて、教皇庁（バチカン市国）とパレスチナ国があります。台湾とクック諸島を別々に含めると、一部のリストは197件になります。

UTCオフセットが最も多いのはどの国ですか？

フランスは海外領土（ヨーロッパ本土、グアドループ、ガイアネ、サン・ピエール・アンド・ミクロン、マヨット、レユニオン、ニューカレドニア、ウォリス・フツナ、フランス領ポリネシア、クロゼ/アデリー、エパルス）のため、技術的には12のUTCオフセットをカバーしています。

どの国がUTC +14を使用していますか？

キリバスのライン諸島—キリチマティ（クリスマス島）を含む。地球上で新しい日ごとに人が住む最初の場所です。

なぜ世界は24時間に分かれていて、それ以上には分かれていないのでしょうか？

24時間制は、1884年の国際子午線会議によって定められました。選択肢は実用的でした。地球の360°回転を15°ステップに分割し、ナビゲーションに便利でした。

夏時間を遵守していない国はどれですか？

アフリカの大部分、赤道圏のアジアのすべて、そして極端な緯度を持つ数少ない国々（ 2014年以降は日本、中国、シンガポール、韓国、アイスランド、ロシア、2016年以降はトルコ）。EUは2026年に季節の変化を止めました。

UTC、GMT、ズールー語の違いは何ですか？

UTCは、1972年にGMTに取って代わった現代の原子ベースのリファレンスです。グリニッジ標準時は現在、英国といくつかの西アフリカ諸国の市民タイムゾーンです。「ズールー語」（ Z ）は、NATOの発音でUTC +0と言います。

EUは2026年にUTC +1/UTC +2を維持しますか？

EUの時計指令は、2026年から春と秋の時計の変更を廃止します。正式にオプトインすると、各国は永続的なUTC +1またはUTC +2のいずれかを選択します。
