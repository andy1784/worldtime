Fair Meeting Rotation Across Time Zones — Scheduling Playbook

���� August 10, 2026  ·  ��� 6 min read  ·  ��� Time Zones, Guide

When a team spans Asia, Europe and the Americas, no single time works for everyone. The standard fix is a rotation: the same weekly call alternates between slots so each region periodically takes the awkward hour. What separates a working rotation from a resentful calendar is fairness bookkeeping.

A Simple Three-Slot Rotation

SlotUTCWho's uncomfortable
A — morning Americas14:00Asia-Pacific evening
B — afternoon Europe09:00US West very early
C — Asia-Pacific friendly23:00Americas late night

Cycle A → B → C → A weekly. Nobody suffers two weeks running.

Making It Stick

  1. Publish the whole rotation in the invite description up front — surprises breed objections.
  2. Set separate reminders per slot in each attendee's local zone; a tool like our world clock helps people internalize their own slot.
  3. Track attendance quality, not just presence: someone dialing in from bed isn't "attending".
  4. Record every session regardless of slot, and keep decisions in writing so missing one never blocks anyone.

When Not to Rotate

If one office is 2 of 20 people, rotating may just push your minority into every bad slot. Alternatives: let the small office stay async, hold two smaller calls, or split the meeting into a recording plus a live Q&A at their chosen time.
