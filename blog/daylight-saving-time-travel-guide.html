What Travelers Need to Know About DST

You've booked your flights, confirmed your hotel, and set your alarm — but did you check if daylight saving time changes during your trip? DST transitions can affect everything from flight times to hotel check-in, and catching you off guard can ruin the first day of your vacation or business trip.

Key DST Dates for 2026

RegionSpring ForwardFall Back
United StatesMarch 8November 1
European UnionMarch 29October 25

The "Gap Week" Problem

The most confusing period for travelers is the week when one region has changed DST but another hasn't:

  • Late March: The US springs forward on March 8, but Europe doesn't change until March 29. For 3 weeks, the US-Europe time difference is 1 hour less than usual.
  • Late October: Europe falls back on October 25, but the US doesn't change until November 1. For a week, the time difference between New York and London is only 4 hours instead of 5.

During these "gap weeks," always double-check the current time difference using our world clock or meeting planner.

Countries That Don't Observe DST

These countries stay on standard time year-round, which simplifies things — but also means the time difference with DST-observing countries changes twice a year:

  • Japan: No DST. Time difference with US/Europe shifts twice yearly.
  • China: No DST. Single time zone (UTC+8) year-round.
  • India: No DST. UTC+5:30 year-round.
  • Most of Africa: No DST observed.
  • Arizona (US): No DST (except Navajo Nation). In summer, Arizona matches Pacific Time.
  • Hawaii (US): No DST. Always UTC-10.

Tips for DST-Smart Travel

  1. Check if your travel dates fall near a DST transition
  2. Verify the time difference for your specific travel dates (not just "usually")
  3. Set world clocks on your phone for both origin and destination
  4. If traveling across a DST change date, confirm all bookings in local time
  5. Allow extra buffer time for connections during DST transition weeks
