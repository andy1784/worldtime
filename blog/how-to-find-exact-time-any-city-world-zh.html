如何查找世界上任何城市的确切时间

You would think that knowing the current time in any city would be trivial in 2026. Your phone shows multiple clocks. Search engines display time zones. But the reality is more complicated — DST changes, half-hour offsets, and outdated databases mean that many sources show the wrong time for weeks or months each year.

Why Time Information Is Often Wrong

Many websites and apps cache time zone rules. When a country changes its DST policy — as Morocco, Turkey, and several US states have done in recent years — outdated databases show incorrect times for months. Some apps still show Venezuela's old UTC-4:30 offset. Others do not account for Nepal's UTC+5:45. The most reliable sources update their time zone databases monthly.

The UTC Offset Method

The most reliable way to know the exact time anywhere is to find the current UTC offset for that location and apply it to the current UTC time. For example, if it is 14:00 UTC and your destination is UTC+9 (Tokyo), the local time is 23:00. If your destination is UTC+5:45 (Kathmandu), the local time is 19:45.

Watch Out for DST Transitions

The most dangerous moments are the weeks around DST transitions. In the US, clocks spring forward on the second Sunday in March and fall back on the first Sunday in November. In Europe, DST starts on the last Sunday in March and ends on the last Sunday in October. During these transition weeks, the time difference between cities can change by one hour.

Half-Hour and Quarter-Hour Offsets

About 15 countries use half-hour or quarter-hour offsets. The most common are India (UTC+5:30), Iran (UTC+3:30), Nepal (UTC+5:45), and parts of Australia (UTC+9:30, UTC+10:30). If your clock app only shows full-hour offsets, it will be wrong for these locations.
