Daylight Saving Time by Country: Complete List for 2026

Daylight saving time (DST) affects time differences between countries twice a year. Here's a comprehensive guide to which countries observe DST and when.

Countries That Observe DST (Northern Hemisphere)

Most change clocks on these dates:

  • United States: 2nd Sunday March → 1st Sunday November
  • Canada: Same as US (most provinces)
  • European Union: Last Sunday March → Last Sunday October
  • United Kingdom: Last Sunday March → Last Sunday October
  • Mexico: 1st Sunday April → Last Sunday October (border cities follow US)

Countries That Observe DST (Southern Hemisphere)

Opposite schedule (spring = fall, fall = spring):

  • Australia: 1st Sunday October → 1st Sunday April (NSW, VIC, SA, TAS, ACT)
  • New Zealand: Last Sunday September → 1st Sunday April
  • Chile: 1st Sunday September → 1st Sunday April
  • Brazil: No longer observes DST (abolished 2019)

Countries That Do NOT Observe DST

  • Japan: JST (UTC+9) year-round
  • China: CST (UTC+8) year-round
  • India: IST (UTC+5:30) year-round
  • Russia: Permanent "winter time" since 2014
  • South Korea: No DST
  • Singapore: No DST
  • UAE: GST (UTC+4) year-round
  • Saudi Arabia: No DST
  • Most of Africa: No DST
  • Most of Southeast Asia: No DST

The DST "Gap" Problem

For about 2 weeks in March and October/November, the US and Europe change clocks at different times. This creates unusual time differences:

  • Mid-March: US springs forward, Europe hasn't yet → US-Europe gap shrinks by 1 hour
  • Late March: Europe springs forward → gap returns to normal
  • Late October: Europe falls back, US hasn't yet → gap grows by 1 hour
  • Early November: US falls back → gap returns to normal

Always double-check time differences during these transition weeks using our world clock.
