There are dozens of "world clock" and "time zone converter" apps out there. Most of them do the same basic thing — show you the current time in a few cities. But when you actually need to coordinate meetings across 5+ time zones, or plan a trip through countries with half-hour offsets, most tools fall short.

We spent three weeks testing 12 popular time zone apps and websites. We evaluated them on five criteria: accuracy of DST handling, speed of use for multi-city lookup, quality of visual presentation, mobile experience, and — this matters — whether they show you overlapping business hours clearly.

Best for Quick Time Checks

The advantage of city-specific pages — like those on World Time Sync — is that search engines send you exactly where you need to go. If you search "what time is it in Nairobi" you land directly on Nairobi's page, not a generic world clock with tiny labels that you have to squint at on your phone.

In testing, we found that lightweight city pages loaded in under a second on mobile, compared to 2–4 seconds for interactive world clock widgets. For a quick check while texting or in a hurry, the difference matters.

Best for Finding Meeting Times

When you need to find a time that works across multiple cities — the visual approach wins. Tools like World Time Buddy and World Time Sync's Meeting Planner use a horizontal timeline where you can see overlapping "green zones of availability" side by side.

Calendly and Doodle solve a different problem: they help you send a scheduling link. They are great for one-on-one meetings but slow when you are trying to decide between 3 possible times for a group call spread across continents.

If your meeting involves people in more than 2 time zones, always check visually before sending the invite. Written-out times (like "9 AM EST / 2 PM GMT / 11 PM JST") cause mistakes.

Best for Reference and Learning

timeanddate.com remains the most complete reference. Their explanations of DST rules, historical time changes, and country-level details are unmatched. The interface is cluttered and slow to navigate, but when you need to understand why a particular offset exists, this is where you go.

FAQ

What is the most accurate time zone app?

Accuracy depends on the underlying data source. All serious tools use the IANA Time Zone Database (also called tz or zoneinfo), which is updated several times per year. Any app using this database is equally accurate — the difference is in usability, not correctness.

Do time zone apps handle DST changes automatically?

Good ones do. The IANA database publishes DST change dates up to a year in advance, and exceptional changes (like a country suddenly switching zones) are typically reflected within days of announcement.

Is there a free time zone tool that works offline?

Limited options exist. Most offline apps bundle a snapshot of the tz database, which becomes stale over time. For occasional offline use, a simple UTC offset table is more practical.
