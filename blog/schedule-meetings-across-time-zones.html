How to Schedule a Meeting Across Time Zones: A Step-by-Step Walkthrough

📅 June 23, 2026  ·  ⏱ 5 min read  ·  🏷 Meetings, Tutorial, Time Zones

Let's walk through a real example. You need to schedule a meeting with three people: you in Berlin (CET), a colleague in Chicago (CST), and a client in Singapore (SGT). Here's how to do it without the usual back-and-forth.

Step 1: List Everyone's Time Zones

  • Berlin: CET (UTC+1) / CEST (UTC+2) in summer
  • Chicago: CST (UTC-6) / CDT (UTC-5) in summer
  • Singapore: SGT (UTC+8), no DST

Step 2: Find the Overlap

Berlin 9 AM CET = 2 AM CST = 4 PM SGT. Too early for Chicago.

Berlin 12 PM CET = 5 AM CST = 7 PM SGT. Still too early for Chicago.

Berlin 2 PM CET = 7 AM CST = 9 PM SGT. This works — Chicago is at the start of their day, Berlin is mid-afternoon, Singapore is evening but still reasonable.

The overlap window is roughly 2–4 PM CET (7–9 AM CST, 9–11 PM SGT).

Step 3: Propose the Time Clearly

Don't just say "2 PM CET." Say:

How about Thursday at 2:00 PM CET (8:00 AM CST / 10:00 PM SGT)?

Including all three time zones eliminates confusion and shows you've done the math.

Step 4: Create the Calendar Invite

Set the meeting time in your local time zone (CET). Google Calendar and Outlook will automatically show each attendee the meeting in their local time. Make sure the time zone field is set correctly — a common mistake is creating the meeting in the wrong time zone.

Step 5: Add a World Clock Link

Include a link to a world clock or time zone converter in the meeting description. This lets attendees double-check the time without asking you.

What If There's No Overlap?

For some combinations (e.g., US West Coast + India), there's almost no business-hour overlap. In these cases:

  • Rotate: Alternate between morning and evening sessions
  • Async: Use a shared doc or Loom video instead of a live meeting
  • Split: Have two shorter meetings, each convenient for one group
