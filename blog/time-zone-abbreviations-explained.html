Time Zone Abbreviations Explained: EST, PST, CET & More (2026)

Time zone abbreviations like EST, PST, and CET are everywhere — but they can be confusing and even ambiguous. Here's a complete guide.

North American Time Zones

  • EST — Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5) / EDT Eastern Daylight Time (UTC-4)
  • CST — Central Standard Time (UTC-6) / CDT Central Daylight Time (UTC-5)
  • MST — Mountain Standard Time (UTC-7) / MDT Mountain Daylight Time (UTC-6)
  • PST — Pacific Standard Time (UTC-8) / PDT Pacific Daylight Time (UTC-7)
  • AKST — Alaska Standard Time (UTC-9) / AKDT (UTC-8)
  • HST — Hawaii Standard Time (UTC-10), no DST

European Time Zones

  • GMT — Greenwich Mean Time (UTC+0) / BST British Summer Time (UTC+1)
  • CET — Central European Time (UTC+1) / CEST (UTC+2)
  • EET — Eastern European Time (UTC+2) / EEST (UTC+3)
  • WET — Western European Time (UTC+0) / WEST (UTC+1)

Asian Time Zones

  • JST — Japan Standard Time (UTC+9), no DST
  • KST — Korea Standard Time (UTC+9), no DST
  • CST — China Standard Time (UTC+8), no DST (also used for US Central!)
  • IST — India Standard Time (UTC+5:30), no DST (also used for Irish/Israeli!)
  • PHT — Philippine Time (UTC+8)
  • ICT — Indochina Time (UTC+7) — Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia

Australian Time Zones

  • AEST — Australian Eastern Standard Time (UTC+10) / AEDT (UTC+11)
  • ACST — Australian Central Standard Time (UTC+9:30) / ACDT (UTC+10:30)
  • AWST — Australian Western Standard Time (UTC+8), no DST

Ambiguous Abbreviations

Some abbreviations mean different things in different contexts:

  • CST: China Standard Time (UTC+8), Cuba Standard Time (UTC-5), or US Central (UTC-6)
  • IST: India Standard Time (UTC+5:30), Irish Standard Time (UTC+1), or Israel Standard Time (UTC+2)
  • BST: British Summer Time (UTC+1) or Bangladesh Standard Time (UTC+6)

Always use the full IANA timezone name (e.g., America/New_York) for clarity in technical contexts.
