Как конвертировать часовые пояса, не теряя рассудка

📅 July 16, 2026  ·  ⏱ 6 min read  ·  🏷 time zones

📅 June 23, 2026  ·  ⏱ 6 min read  ·  🏷 Time Zones, Conversion, Tutorial

Time zone math is one of those things that sounds simple until you actually have to do it. "India is 10.5 hours ahead of New York" — okay, so 3 PM in New York is... 1:30 AM in India? Or 2:30 AM? And does DST change this?

Let's break it down step by step.

The Basic Math

To convert between time zones, you need to know the UTC offset for each zone. Then you subtract:

Time in City B = Time in City A − UTC offset of A + UTC offset of B

Example: Convert 3:00 PM EST (UTC-5) to IST (UTC+5:30):

3:00 PM − (−5) + (+5:30) = 3:00 PM + 5 + 5:30 = 1:30 AM (next day)

The half-hour offset is what trips people up. India (UTC+5:30), Iran (UTC+3:30), Afghanistan (UTC+4:30), Myanmar (UTC+6:30), and parts of Australia (UTC+9:30, UTC+10:30) all have 30-minute offsets. Newfoundland (UTC-3:30) too.

The Easy Way: Use a Tool

Honestly, for anything beyond simple conversions, use a tool. Our world clock lets you compare multiple cities at once. Google "time in Tokyo" and it shows you the current time. For future times, tools like our meeting planner handle the math for you.

But it's still useful to understand the basics, especially for quick mental math.

Mental Math Shortcuts

For common conversions, memorize these:

  • US East Coast to UK: Add 5 hours (or 4 during DST overlap)
  • US West Coast to UK: Add 8 hours (or 7 during DST overlap)
  • US East Coast to India: Add 10.5 hours (or 9.5 during DST)
  • US East Coast to Japan: Add 14 hours (or 13 during DST)
  • UK to India: Add 5.5 hours
  • UK to Australia (East): Add 10–11 hours

DST Complications

The offsets above change when DST is active. The US observes DST from March to November. Europe observes it from late March to late October. Australia observes it from October to April (opposite hemisphere).

This means the time difference between two cities can change 2–4 times per year. A US-UK call might be 5 hours apart for 7 months and 4 hours apart for 3 months, with 2 transition weeks at different offsets.

Always check whether DST is active in both locations before calculating.

Common Mistakes

Forgetting the date change: When you add hours and cross midnight, the date changes. 10 PM + 5 hours = 3 AM the next day. This matters for scheduling — a meeting at 10 PM Friday in New York is 3 AM Saturday in London.

Mixing up the direction: "Ahead" and "behind" get confusing. India is "ahead" of the US (later in the day), which means you add hours to US time to get India time. But India is "behind" the US in terms of calendar date when it's evening in the US.

Ignoring half-hour offsets: India (UTC+5:30), not UTC+5. Iran (UTC+3:30), not UTC+3. These 30-minute differences matter.

Assuming DST is universal: Most of Asia, Africa, and South America don't observe DST. If you're converting between New York and Mumbai, the offset changes twice a year (when the US switches). Between New York and Tokyo, it doesn't (Japan doesn't observe DST).
